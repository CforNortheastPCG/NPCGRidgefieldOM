## Narrative / copy (paste-ready)

**Neighborhood overview (subtitle: "Bridgeport's Standout Coastal Pocket"):**
> Black Rock is the standout neighborhood within Bridgeport — a historic, harbor-front
> pocket on the city's far west side that runs right up to the Fairfield town line. Built
> around sea-captains' homes and a genuinely walkable main street, its spine is **Fairfield
> Avenue**: a lively corridor of independent restaurants, bars, coffee shops, galleries,
> and music venues anchored by neighborhood staples like Taco Loco, The Sitting Room, and
> Park City Music Hall. Residents walk to dinner, take in the half-mile waterfront
> promenade at **St. Mary's-By-The-Sea**, and keep boats at Captain's Cove Seaport on
> Black Rock Harbor.
>
> For an investor, the appeal is value, not prestige — and that's the point. Black Rock
> isn't Fairfield or Westport, but it offers a genuinely walkable, waterfront lifestyle at
> a fraction of Gold Coast prices, which keeps a steady, year-round pool of renters.

**Old-money / character angle ("Old Fairfield County, on the Water"):**
> Black Rock is old Fairfield County. Long before it was annexed into Bridgeport it was a
> colonial seaport, and that maritime, old-money character never left — sea-captain
> Victorians line the side streets, the harbor is still full of boats, and the **Black Rock
> Yacht Club (est. 1879)** has anchored the waterfront for nearly a century and a half. It
> delivers that Gold Coast lifestyle at a meaningful discount to neighboring Fairfield and
> Westport — the quiet value gap that underpins durable demand and steady rent growth.

**About Black Rock (bullets):** historic waterfront enclave on the Fairfield line ·
Fairfield Ave walkable corridor · St. Mary's-By-The-Sea / Black Rock Harbor / Captain's
Cove · value-driven renter demand vs Fairfield & Westport · Metro-North New Haven Line
(Fairfield Metro & Bridgeport stations) → Grand Central · I-95 Exits 24/25 + Route 1.
